AGI vs ASI vs Embodied AI

Three related terms describe different dimensions of advanced artificial intelligence. Understanding the distinctions matters when evaluating claims about robotics and physical intelligence.

Research explainer · November 5, 2026 · Robot Embodiment Editorial

Artificial general intelligence (AGI)

AGI describes a hypothetical or aspirational level of AI with broad competence across a wide range of tasks and domains, rather than narrow specialization. There is no single universally accepted operational definition or agreed test for AGI. Researchers disagree about the required breadth, autonomy, learning ability and level of performance.

Artificial superintelligence (ASI)

ASI describes a hypothetical system whose capabilities substantially exceed human performance across many important cognitive domains. It is generally discussed as a possible future capability level beyond AGI. It should not be used as a synonym for a large language model, a powerful specialist model or a robot that excels at one task.

Embodied AI

Embodied AI refers to intelligent agents that perceive and act through a body in a physical or simulated environment. The body may be a humanoid, robot arm, quadruped, mobile manipulator or simulated agent. The defining emphasis is the closed loop between sensing, decision-making and action.

Physical AI

Physical AI is a broad industry and research term for AI systems that interact with the physical world, often through robots, autonomous machines or other cyber-physical systems. Its usage varies across organizations and publications; it is not a standardized intelligence level.

How the concepts relate

AGI and ASI describe breadth or level of capability. Embodied AI describes a mode of learning and acting grounded in interaction with an environment. Physical AI describes an applied technology category. A system could be embodied without being general, general without controlling a physical body, or highly capable in a digital domain without being superintelligent in robotics.

Embodied AGI is the research idea that broad general capabilities could be grounded in physical interaction. It raises questions about whether experience across bodies and environments can produce more transferable models of objects, dynamics, causality and action. This is an active research direction, not an established route or guaranteed outcome.

Why these distinctions matter in robotics

A robot's successful task completion is evidence about a particular task, hardware configuration and evaluation setup. It is not, by itself, evidence of AGI or ASI. Strong claims require broad testing across novel tasks, objects, environments and embodiments, plus transparent reporting of training data, human engineering and failure rates.

Researchers studying general-purpose physical intelligence often investigate vision-language-action models, robot foundation models, world models, continual learning, hierarchical planning, reinforcement learning, imitation learning and cross-embodiment transfer. These are components or research approaches—not interchangeable definitions of AGI.
